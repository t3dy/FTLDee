# FTLDee — Historical Provenance

Every event in the game carries a `historicalStatus` tag. This document explains the
categories and provides the primary historical grounding for the Dee vertical slice.

## Status Categories

| Status          | Meaning                                                        |
|-----------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| `documented`    | Attested in the historical record                              |
| `plausible`     | Historically consistent reconstruction with no direct evidence |
| `contested`     | Subject of historiographical debate                            |
| `counterfactual`| Deliberate departure from the historical record                |
| `anachronistic` | Deliberately out-of-time (player-created possibility)          |

The game should never silently conflate these categories. A player should be able to
understand, if they choose to look, what kind of historical claim the game is making.

## John Dee: Historical Grounding

### Biographical Anchors (Documented)

**Mathematical Preface (1570)**
Dee wrote the Mathematical Preface to the first English translation of Euclid, establishing
his reputation as a leading mathematical mind in England. *Source: documented.*

**Library at Mortlake**
Dee maintained one of the largest private libraries in England at his house in Mortlake,
Surrey. It was a working library for scholarship and consultation. *Source: documented.*

**Royal Consultations**
Elizabeth I consulted Dee on astronomical and occult matters on multiple documented
occasions, including his examination of an unusual comet. *Source: documented.*

**1555 Arrest**
Dee was arrested under Mary I in connection with an occult consultation involving Princess
Elizabeth. The exact nature of the alleged activity (astrology vs. spirit conjuring) is
uncertain. *Source: documented; characterization contested.*

**Catholic Phase under Mary**
Dee was ordained a Catholic priest under Mary I and had genuine Catholic intellectual
connections from his Cambridge training. His Catholicism had intellectual roots, not merely
political convenience. *Source: Parry, documented.*

**Shifting Court Fortunes**
Dee's fortunes were repeatedly contingent on court factions; his temporary successes
could result from one faction finding his expertise useful and then abandoning him when
its agenda changed. *Source: Parry/Clulee interpretation, contested historiography.*

**1583 Continental Departure**
Dee left England in 1583 with Edward Kelley and Albert Laski, traveling to the Continent
and eventually reaching Rudolf II's court in Prague. *Source: documented.*

**Angelic Conversations**
From approximately 1581, Dee conducted an extensive series of angelic consultations using
a scryer (initially Barnabas Saul, then Kelley). The logs survive. *Source: documented.*

### Historiographical Interpretations (Contested)

**Parry's Dee** (Glyn Parry, *The Arch-Conjuror of England*)
Dee repeatedly made navigational errors at political transitions, especially Mary→Elizabeth.
His Catholic past created ongoing political vulnerability under Elizabeth. His court position
was dependent on shifting factional support rather than durable institutional patronage.
This reading emphasizes Dee's political missteps.

**Pumfrey's Dee**
Dee's patronage was extensive but repeatedly frustrated because the English court was
structurally reluctant to sustain speculative natural philosophy institutionally. Elizabeth
liked Dee personally while failing to build his career materially. The frustration was
systemic rather than solely due to Dee's errors.

**The Yates/Rosicrucian Connection**
Frances Yates argued for a direct Dee → Rosicrucianism → Palatinate connection. This
interpretation is now substantially revised by later scholarship. The game should model
it as one possible historical hypothesis, not objective truth.

### Counterfactual Scenarios

These are explicitly not history. They emerge from player choices.

**The Elizabethan Dee**: Strong English institutional patronage that historically did not materialize.

**The Imperial Dee**: Sustained Rudolf II patronage; historically Dee was eventually expelled from Prague.

**The Publishing Dee**: Dee builds a printing network more extensive than he historically managed.

**The Ottoman Dee** (from Melvin-Koushki): Dee finds a patron in the Ottoman Sultan Murad III,
who was historically interested in occult-scientific knowledge. This is an explicit counterfactual
based on Melvin-Koushki's scholarly thought experiment.

## Key Historical Figures in the Vertical Slice

### Elizabeth I
- Consulted Dee on multiple occasions (documented)
- Personal favor reportedly high; material support and institutional backing much lower (Pumfrey)
- Her relationship with Dee is best understood as patronizing an extraordinary individual
  without building durable institutional support (contested interpretation)

### William Cecil (Lord Burghley)
- Secretary of State, Lord Treasurer; cautious Protestant pragmatist
- Compatible with Dee's mathematical/navigation/imperial projects
- Cautious toward occult work; preferred useful, politically defensible expertise

### Robert Dudley (Earl of Leicester)
- Elizabeth's favorite; associated with militant Protestantism and Protestant occultism
- More sympathetic to occult and natural philosophy than Cecil
- Part of the Sidney/Leicester cultural circle that included crypto-Hermetic tendencies

### Francis Walsingham
- Principal Secretary; intelligence chief
- Documented use of mathematical and cipher expertise for intelligence work
- Dee's relationship with intelligence networks is plausible but exact details are contested

### Philip Sidney
- Leicester's nephew; linked to the broader Protestant intellectual culture
- Continental networks; interest in natural philosophy and occult speculation
- Killed at Zutphen 1586 (documented)

### Edward Kelley
- Historical scryer employed by Dee from 1582 (documented)
- Also claimed alchemical expertise (documented; results contested)
- Political intelligence was poor; could mislead Dee's decisions (contested interpretation)
- His "dual-class" (alchemy + scrying) is mechanically important

### Jane Dee (née Fromond)
- Married Dee 1578 (documented)
- Managed the household at Mortlake during Dee's absences
- Household stability is closely associated with her role
