## 1. research/alchemy/RAMPLING.md

**SERIOUS**

1. `RAMPLING.md:73-75` "omits alchemy altogether, which Rampling calls 'puzzling' and **explains by**
   alchemy's tradition of secrecy and the danger shown by de Lannoy's fall (R2011 143–144)".
   R2011 144: the omission "is therefore puzzling, and **may reflect** the fact that alchemical
   writing ... maintained a strong tradition of secrecy"; de Lannoy is given as a reason
   practitioners "had to tread carefully". A suggestion made into an explanation (lost hedge).
   Fix: "and suggests it may reflect alchemy's tradition of secrecy".
2. `RAMPLING.md:102-103` "Kelley's knowledge 'he owed ... to Dee' (R2012a 503)". R2012a 503: "In
   general, however, **the evidence suggests** that he owed such knowledge to Dee". Hedge dropped.
   Fix: "the evidence, Rampling says, suggests that Kelley 'owed such knowledge to Dee'".
3. `RAMPLING.md:103-104` "Dee's copy of the Bosome Book was 'a likely source' for the *Viaticum*".
   R2012a 505: "**If Dee had a copy of the Book with him in Třeboň**, this must be considered a
   likely source for the extracts ... in the form of the Viaticum". The condition is dropped and
   the copy's existence asserted (RIPLEY.md §6 correctly calls Dee's ownership her inference).
   Fix: restore the "if".
4. `RAMPLING.md:140-141` "He forged authority: the *Work of Dunstan* ... and the gliding-fire token
   written into it". Stated as fact. EF 312 speaks of "Kelley's **probable** role in adapting the
   Work of Dunstan" and frames the forgery as a rhetorical question ("How better to set the stage
   ... than by writing a known, replicable effect into a forged medieval treatise?"); R2012a 503
   n. 38 says only that it is "conceivable". RIPLEY.md §8.5 and DEEALCHEMYLABORS record this
   correctly; this file contradicts them. Fix: "Rampling argues Kelley probably adapted the *Work
   of Dunstan* ... (EF 312; only 'conceivable' in R2012a 503 n. 38)".

**EDITS**

   his alchemical reading is the list of July 1556".
2. `RAMPLING.md:138-139` "His most famous demonstration was making the mercury of gold before the
   emperor in fifteen minutes (EF 286)". EF 286: what "contemporaries **later recalled**", "most

   17th c." Clucas 2021 pdf 3 says "predominant ... throughout the sixteenth and early seventeenth

## 2. research/alchemy/RIPLEY.md

**SERIOUS**

1. `RIPLEY.md:180-181` "The item 'Ripley, anglice' is, Rampling argues, really Norton's
   *Ordinal* (R2012a 500 n. 14)". Garbled. R2012a 500 n. 14: Roberts & Watson suggest the item is
   the "Ordinale secretum" in DM10 (Trinity R.14.38); "However, this [DM10] is actually Thomas
   Norton's Ordinal of alchemy, which might better correspond to the item 'Norton anglice' on Dee's
   1556 list." She rejects the identification of "Ripley, anglice" and leaves it unidentified. Fix:
   "Roberts and Watson identify 'Ripley, anglice' with DM10; Rampling notes DM10 is Norton's
   *Ordinal*, which better matches the separate entry 'Norton anglice' (R2012a 500 n. 14)".
2. `RIPLEY.md:166-167` Mai versified the *Liber* for Rudolf "at Kelley's instigation" (R2012a
   501–502). R2012a 501: "**apparently** at Kelley's instigation". Fix: restore "apparently" (Mai's
