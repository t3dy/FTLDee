## A. England, 1540s–1583: reader, collector, householder

**c. 1542–46, Cambridge.** Parry: Dee's "occult studies began at Cambridge", where alchemy appeared
a natural continuation of Aristotle's *On Coming-to-be*; Cecil, Smith and Eden pursued the stone
there (Parry 9). Clulee: before 1556 there is "little indication of alchemy in Dee's library", which
was mostly classics, mathematics and astronomy (Clulee 2005 199). **DISAGREE** on when the interest
begins (Parry 9 vs Clulee 2005 198–199); Clulee also records Dee's first alchemical text acquired
in 1551 (Clulee 2005 198).

**1556, the reading burst.** Dee's list "Authores Alchymici quos perlegi anno 1556, a mense Julij"
(CCC Oxford MS 191) has 55 titles: Geber, Bacon, Lull, Arnald, Ortolanus, Ripley, Norton, Dastin
and anonymous works (Clulee 2005 199; R2012a 498 n. 1; R2011 143). Parry places Dee that July in
Bishop Bonner's household, trying to reconcile 55 tracts (Parry 42). Earlier in 1556 he borrowed
manuscripts from Peterhouse, St John's, Queen's and Oriel and bought "five manuscripts of astronomy,
astrology and alchemy" and a Roger Bacon alchemical manuscript at the Leland sale (Parry 42). He read new printed compendia
(Petreius 1541; Cyriacus Jacob 1550) as well as manuscripts (EF 207). Not all listed items were
owned (R2012a 498).

**1550s–60s, salvaging monastic books.** Dee gathered manuscripts from colleges and former
religious houses, St Albans and St Augustine's, Canterbury among them (EF 202), including the great
Lullian compendia CCC 244 and Mellon 12 (EF 169). 16 May 1561: gave William Blomfild, ex-monk
alchemist, a Polychronicon from St Augustine's (EF 203).

**1559.** Received Pantheus's *Voarchadumia* as a gift from the Venetian alchemist Giovanni Battista
Agnello in London; his annotations to it are perhaps his earliest alchemical use of the monad
(Clulee 2005 201). Dee adopted Pantheus's term "Voarchadumia" (EF 231).

**1561–64, the Continent.** Interest in Paracelsus from the early 1560s; 23 April 1563 visit to
Gesner in Zürich; by 1583 he held 92 editions of Paracelsus (Clulee 2005 200). Itinerary Antwerp,
Louvain, Pressburg, Venice, Urbino, Padua, Rome; "possible that he also took advantage of meeting
alchemists" (Clulee 2005 201). Parry is firmer: "Along the way he studied alchemical books,
consulted alchemists" (Parry 52). **DISAGREE** in confidence (Clulee "possible" vs Parry as fact).

**1564, the *Monas hieroglyphica*.** Written and printed in Antwerp in the house of the printer
Willem Silvius, dedicated to Maximilian II (Clucas 2017 1–2). "Probably the first alchemical work
which focused exclusively on the diagrammatic representation of the alchemical process" (Clucas
2017 1). The monad yields the planetary/metallic signs; a lunar series (Saturn, Jupiter, Moon,
Mercury) of whitening and a solar series (Mars, Venus, Sun); seven stages of "astronomia inferior";
the egg diagram (Clulee 2005 206–208). Its denary proportion rests on the Pythagorean tetraktys via
Trithemius (Clucas 2010 163 [pdf 17]). Dee wanted the proportions of the printed sign kept by
"those wishing to bear it on rings and seals" (Clucas 2017 16). Elizabeth read and defended it at
Greenwich shortly after publication (Clucas 2017 2; Parry 226). Clulee: "no evidence that Dee had
any practical experience with alchemy before 1564" (Clulee 2005 211).

**1564–65, the de Lannoy affair.** Elizabeth set up Cornelius de Lannoy at Somerset House with a
£120 pension; he promised £33,000 of gold a year (Parry 76; EF 204). Parry argues Dee's *Monas*
(and its nod to the "Voarchadumicus") introduced the Queen and Cecil to ideas that made them accept
de Lannoy (Parry 74–75); this is Parry's interpretation. Parry has de Lannoy's furnaces moved to the Tower in
August 1566 and de Lannoy put in a cell in early 1567 (Parry 77–78); Rampling dates his
"consignment to the Tower" to 1565 (R2011 144). **DISAGREE** on the date. Parry: the Court wanted transmutation, and "Dee's practical alchemical skills proved ordinary"
(Parry 71).

**c. 1564 onward, Mortlake laboratories.** Only after settling at Mortlake did Dee have a place to
practise (Clulee 2005 211). His *Compendious Rehearsall* (1592) speaks of "my three laboratories,
serving for Pyrotechnia" as "an appendix practical" to his library, over twenty years and £200 of
building, vessels "of earth, ... of metall, ... of glass" (Clulee 2005 212). "By 1583 he had built
three laboratories" (Clulee 1988 178); "three alchemical laboratories by 1583" (Parry 85); "three
alchemical workshops" (R2011 143).

**1566/7–1581, Roger Cook.** Joined at 14, tended the stills (Clulee 2005 212). 28 Dec 1579: "I
revealed to Roger Cook the great secret of the elixir of the salt" (Fenton 7). Left in Sept 1581
after "hot words", barred from "my philosophical dealing with Mr Henrik"; Dee promised him "some
pretty alchemical experiments: whereupon he might honestly live" (Fenton 14–15). **DISAGREE (date
slip):** Clulee 2005 212 says Cook stayed "until 1580"; Clulee 1988 178 and Fenton 14–15 give 1581.
Cook returned in Sept 1600 promising help "in the practices chemical" and was distilling by
November (Clulee 2005 212; Fenton 288–289).

**1567–68, alchemy as courtly pitch.** German presentation copy of the *Monas* for Maximilian,
27 Jan 1567; revised *Propaedeumata* (Jan 1568) with alchemical additions from the *Monas*,
presented to Cecil; Feb 1568 "very gracious talk" with Elizabeth about a "great secret" via
Grudius, which came to nothing (Parry 79–80).

**1568.** Dee's English verse *Testament*, "ad Joannem Gwynn transmissum", written into Harley 2407
(EF 324).

**c. 1570.** The *Mathematicall Praeface* calls alchemy "terrestrial astrology" (Parry 9) yet
omits alchemy from its scheme of sciences, which Rampling finds "puzzling" (R2011 143–144). Humphrey
Gilbert's proposed Academy would have had alchemists file annual reports of successes and failures
(Parry 84–85).

**1571, Lorraine.** Dee travelled to Lorraine for specialised glass, returning with a "great cart
lading of purposely made vessells" (Clulee 2005 212); Parry: with Thomas Powle, on a royal passport,
some vessels for the Queen; Dee returned ill and the Queen sent her physicians with Mary Sidney
(Parry 86–87). Parry links the trip to the Sidneys' alchemical hopes (Parry 86) **[Parry's
inference; "no evidence about it survives", Parry 87]**. Philip Sidney and Edward Dyer "studied
alchemy ... under Dee" (Parry 86).

**1570s, reputation among practitioners.** Hugh Plat recorded Dee's recipe for a lamp oil for gentle
heating; "Mr John Dee his black lute" (a cement for vessels) circulated (Parry 87–88).

**1576–78.** Commissioner for assaying Frobisher's ore, with Agnello (Clulee 2005 213).

**1580–82, contacts.** 12 Aug 1580: letters and "the mercurii mensitam or sigillum planetarum" from
Dr Andrew Hels (Clulee 2005 200, citing Fenton 14). May 1582: Robert Gardner of Shrewsbury brings
news of the stone "divinely revealed to him" and declares "a certain great philosophical secret"
(Clulee 2005 200–201). Gardner was Dee's alchemical assistant after Cook (Fenton, Biographical
Guide, DeeChunks p. 342; printed page not verified).

**1581, the notebook (Bodleian Rawlinson D.241, 22 June–6 Oct).** 24 July: 3 oz silver in aqua
fortis, a reaction that "seems to have almost got out of hand", struck down with rain water and "strawberry water
of the sea" (Clulee 2005 197–198, 213). Precise weights and times, adjustments of heat (Clulee 2005
213). Quick mercury pressed from a sublimate "betwene my fingers"; dose of water kept small "as
Riplay in philortium warnes of" (R2012a 500; EF 296). 1 Sept: a balneum that would no longer
ascend (Fenton 14). Separately, undated in Clulee: an assistant Harry Waters "went away malcontent"
(Rawlinson D.241 fol. 3, via Clulee 2005 212 n. 71). Parry reads the notebook as
matching Gilbert's Academy-style reporting, with planetary timings (Parry 85). Parry also says Dee
had "some success in alchemical projection with salts of metals" in this period (Parry 146).
**DISAGREE** on Dee's skill: "no armchair alchemist" (R2012a 500) / Dee "seems to have been a
rather indifferent alchemist" (Clulee 2005 214) / "ordinary" (Parry 71).

**1582–83, Kelley, the book, the powder.** Kelley (as "Talbot") became scryer 10 March 1582 (Parry
147). Dee believed the 1572 supernova foreshadowed Kelley's revelation of the stone (Parry 147).
Kelley left for Blockley on 22 Nov 1582 and returned (actions resumed 23 March 1583) with a book, a
scroll in "strange characters" and a phial of red powder, found by guidance of "a spirituall
Creature"; Dee calls it the Book of Dunstan (Whitby 43–45). **DISAGREE** on the book: "almost
certainly written by Kelly himself" (Fenton 61 n. 9); possibly a copy of a treatise attributed to
Dunstan (Whitby 44–45, reporting Fell Smith); Rampling identifies the *Work of Dunstan* as a Latin
reworking of Ripley's *Accurtations* and argues Kelley's "probable role in adapting" it (EF 290,
312), more cautiously in 2012 ("conceivable", R2012a 503 n. 38).

**1583, copper.** Dee leased a Devonshire copper mine and hired Thomas Hoke on 10 July 1583, who
left within two weeks (Parry 89); Parry links this to Medley's "transmutation" of iron into copper
(Parry 88–89) **[Parry's inference]**.

**1583, the library catalogue and departure.** The 1583 catalogue groups "Chemici libri &c.
compacti" and "Chemici latinè non compacti" (Clulee 2005 199). Dee closed "his domestic alchemical
research institute", took about 800 printed books and almost 100 manuscripts, borrowed £400 from
Nicholas Fromoundes on the security of the house and goods, and left on 21 September (Parry 170–171;
R2012a 500; c. 800 books, R2012a 504). Books marked "T" in the catalogue may be those meant to travel
(R2012a 504 n. 49).

## B. Poland and Prague, 1583–1586

**Feb 1584, Lasko.** Łaski expected Kelley's alchemy to fix his finances; Kelley failed; asked how
to use the red powder, the angels set worldly treasures below "the wisdom that judgeth NATURE"
(Parry 174), and Parry's narration explains that Kelley
had failed because the powder was not the whole philosopher's stone (Parry 173–174; Parry's words,
not the angels'). 21 April 1584: Kelley
believed his projections proved it was (Parry 175).

**Aug 1584, Prague.** Lodged with Tadeáš Hájek; angelic actions began in his house, whose study walls
were painted with six alchemical vessels (Parry 180). **DISAGREE** on Hájek's office: Parry calls
him personal physician to Charles V, Maximilian II and Rudolf (Parry 180); Clulee "an alchemical
adviser to Emperor Rudolph II" (Clulee 2005 201); Rampling, following Purš, says that is an error:
"In fact he provided medical care to some of the servants at court" (EF 292 n. 31). The *Monas*
reached Rudolf in 1584 via a Spanish ambassador (Clucas 2017 2).

**1584–85, the angels' alchemy.** The angels promised "Adam's alchemy", the "medicine of God" that
would heal nature; alchemy was "the last subject covered in the angels' lessons" (Harkness 195–196,
203). The angels' alchemy "did not correspond exactly" to Dee's practised alchemy (Harkness 201).
Jan 1585 Kelley sees alchemical visions; the angels' recipe "Darr" uses wine-barrel residue to tint
metals golden (Parry 184–185). Dee later wrote that he was "chief Governor of our Philosophical
proceedings" only until March 1585 (Parry 186).

**1585–86.** Dee asked Elizabeth to send Thomas Digges to witness their success; Kelley sent a sample
of transmuted metal (Parry 186, 192). May 1586: Dee visited the glasshouses at "Valkenaw" for
alchemical vessels (Parry 192). Exiled from Prague May 1586 at the nuncio's instance; Rožmberk
secured Třeboň (EF 292).

## C. Třeboň, 14 Sept 1586–March 1589

(Arrival date: EF 292; Parry 196.)

- **From Oct 1586** Dee notes successful processes, expeditions for vessels, Kelley at Rožmberk's
  Prague laboratories (Parry 197).
- **19 Dec 1586** Kelley's public projection before Edward Garland: one grain to 1¼ oz of mercury,
  almost 1 oz of gold; Dee and Kelley divided the gold, the crucible went to Garland (Parry 197;
  Clulee 2005 214).
- **26 Mar and 16 Sept 1587** visits of Karl von Biberstein, mint master (R2012a 502).
- **18 Apr 1587** a spirit "Ben" tells Kelley at the still that he had guarded the powder and the
  Book of Dunstan (Whitby 443, note to 63a); the cross-matching pact follows, with the threat that the
  powder would become useless (Parry 198).
- **Summer 1587** Kelley works with mercury in Rožmberk's Třeboň mansion; Dee shares the
  distillation and takes astrological readings; they divide the "mercury animal" (Parry 200). 28
  Sept 1587 Kelley brings weights to split the "[mercury] Animall" (R2012a 506). **[inference]** the
  note "the two earths (left of the 28 oz. of the Animal mercury)" that Clulee lists among Mortlake
  practice (Clulee 2005 212) is cited from Fenton 229, which falls in the Třeboň period.
- **28–29 Oct 1587** "Jo. Carp. did begyn to make furnaces over the gate &c.: & he vsed of my rownd
  bricks" (R2012a 506; EF 293 n. 33; Fenton 231). **DISAGREE:** Parry: "In late October Rozmberk
  built additional furnaces" (Parry 201).
- **Late 1587** they follow a regime from Kelley's writings and two books, Zacaire's (translated by
  Dee from French) and Kelley's extracts from the Book of Dunstan (Parry 201). Dee translated "the
  Opuscule of Daniel Zeccaire" for Kelley (R2012a 503).
- **12 Dec 1587** Kelley's spirit lamp falls over, "the glass being not stayed with bricks about it",
  burning Zacaire with the athanor, Rouillasch's third book of waters, the *Angelicum Opus* in
  pictures, the Budweis man's conclusions, and the 40-leaf "Extractiones Dunstani"; the Book of
  Dunstan itself, on the bed, survived (Fenton 231–232). **[inference]** Whitby's printed date
  "12 December 1597" for this fire (Whitby 159–160) is a slip for 1587.
- **8 Feb 1588** sericon distilled "according as in tyme past & of late he hard [sic] of me out of
  Riplay"; jointly signed note "J:D. E:K." with 2 lb sericon → 4 oz red oil and a sketch of the
  crystalline ring (R2012a 505; EF 297–299; Fenton 233). **DISAGREE:** Parry: Kelley "distilled
  antimony ... which Dee read aloud" (Parry 201); see `RIPLEY.md` §8.4.
- **10 May 1588** "E. K. did open the great secret to me, God be thanked!" (EF 284 epigraph; Fenton
  235). Rampling: a sign of Dee's demotion from patron to assistant (EF 299–300).
- **July–Aug 1588** Edward Dyer visits, snubs Dee; Dee sees "the divine water" and calls Kelley "the
  magnificent master" (Parry 202). Sept 1588: Kelley excludes Dee from his work (Parry 202).
- **Dec 1588** Kelley gives Dee the mercurial water; Dee gives Kelley his perspective glass, which
  went to Rudolf's Kunstkammer (Parry 202–203).
- **25–30 Jan 1589** Nicolaus Mai visits Třeboň (Fenton 238: "25 Jan. Mr Maius came"; R2012a 502
  gives 26 January).
- **4 Feb 1589** Dee hands Kelley "the powder, the books, the glass and the bone for the L.
  Rosenberg", receiving a signed discharge (Fenton 238; Parry 203).
- **March 1589** Dee leaves, "disheartened by his lack of fortune and under pressure by Rožmberk"
  (R2012a 506; EF 300–301).

## D. England again, 1589–1608

- **Bremen, Oct 1589** Gawin Smith visits for several days (R2012a 505).
- **Dec 1589** Back at Mortlake (rented from Fromoundes) Dee finds "the alchemical laboratories
  bare, and over five hundred books missing" (Parry 206). **DISAGREE** on the 1583 spoliation: the
  "hoary legend" of a mob is rejected; Dee's associates and creditors took books, Adrian Gilbert
  "seized Dee's alchemical instruments and remaining alchemical books", and Fromoundes sold the
  equipment, including the Lorraine vessels, at about 80 per cent of value (Parry 171–172).
  `docs/SYSTEMS_V2.md` §5 already cites Håkansson 31–33 to the same effect (not re-read here).
  Commissioners appointed 9 Dec 1589 to trace the missing books and materials (Parry 205).
- **1590** Walsingham consults Dee on two London alchemists (Parry 206). July: Dee argues with
  Whitgift "of the truth of Sir Edward Kelley his Alchemy" and the next day begins "my alchemical
  exercises" (Parry 212). 31 July: lends Richard Cavendish Zacaire's twelve letters under oath of
  secrecy (Fenton 250; Parry 214). Nov–Dec: Elizabeth sends for Dee, perhaps for an
  experiment; on 16 Dec Cavendish brings word that Dee should "do what I would in philosophy and
  alchemy, and none should check, control or molest me" (Parry 214–215).
- **1591** Rabbards prints the *Compound*, praising Dee's *Monas* (R2012a 506); the *Monas* appears at
  Frankfurt, "pirated" according to Clulee (2005 211), republished by Dee himself according to Parry
  (215): **DISAGREE**. Kelley arrested: "In April 1591" (EF 284); the day before Burghley's letter of
  12 May (Parry 216): **DISAGREE** on the month. Sir Thomas Jones
  offers Dee a Welsh castle and money for his alchemy (Parry 216). Parry notes that the St Cross
  mastership could easily have housed Dee's library and alchemical laboratories (Parry 218).
- **1592** *Compendious Rehearsall* lists embezzled "alchemical vessels and distillations"; Dee dreams
  of St Cross as a European centre for alchemy, near the Sussex glasshouses (Parry 87, 235).
- **1593–94** Francis Nichols pays £300 to learn "the conclusion of fixing and taming of silver";
  Lord Willoughby gives £20 for the stone; Thomas Webb, Dee's alchemical colleague, is jailed for
  alchemical coining, and the Master of the Mint seals Webb's chest at Dee's house (Parry 239–240).
- **1595/1597** Dee annotates the *Compound* in e Musaeo 63: dated 21 Dec 1595 (R2012a 500) or "dated
  1597" (CRC 155). **DISAGREE** (see `RIPLEY.md` §8.3).
- **1589 interview** Hugh Plat found Dee evasive, with stock textual answers (Clulee 2005 214, citing
  an unpublished Harkness paper).
- **1600** Roger Cook returns for "the practices chemical" (Clulee 2005 212).
- **4 Dec 1607–21 Jan 1608** last recorded series of chemical processes (Clulee 2005 198; Ashmole
  1486 art. v). Parry: after the angels promised the Book of Dunstan and the stone in July 1607, Dee
  began alchemical work at Mortlake in December "which somehow petered out in January 1608" (Parry
  269). Dee's transcription in Ashmole 1486 pt. 5 glosses sericon as "red leade" + antimony; EF 340
  and R2014 28 call it "early seventeenth-century", but CRC 198 (35.12) dates that manuscript
  1575–1600: **DISAGREE** on the date, so it is not placed in 1607–08 here. In 1608 he hoped to join Hartmann's "alchemical college" at Marburg (Parry 270).

## E. Afterlives

- The *Monas* was reprinted in Zetzner's *Theatrum chemicum* (1602, 1659) with errors (Forshaw 2005
  248 n. 6); Libavius used its proportions for the ground plan of his ideal laboratory (Forshaw 2005
  267).
- Ashmole copied Ripley's retraction from "a vellum Manuscript of Dr. Dees" and printed Dee's
  *Testament* (EF 324).
- John Winthrop Jr signed books with Dee's monas and lost a Norton manuscript "formerly in the
  possession of famous Dr. Dee" (*Past & Present* 241 [2018], Calis, Rampling et al., pdf 11–12).

## F. Summary of open disagreements

1. When Dee's alchemy began: Cambridge (Parry 9) vs 1551/1556 (Clulee 2005 198–199).
2. Practice before 1564: none evidenced (Clulee 2005 211); consulting alchemists 1563 (Parry 52).
3. Skill: hands-on (R2012a 500) vs "seems to have been" indifferent (Clulee 2005 214) vs ordinary but some projection
   success (Parry 71, 146).
4. Roger Cook's departure: 1580 (Clulee 2005 212) vs 1581 (Clulee 1988 178; Fenton 14–15).
5. Book of Dunstan: Kelley's forgery (Fenton 61 n. 9), a copy of an older tract (Whitby 44–45),
   a Ripleian reworking Kelley probably adapted (EF 312) vs only "conceivable" (R2012a 503 n. 38).
6. Hájek's status (Parry 180; Clulee 2005 201 vs EF 292 n. 31).
7. Who built the Třeboň furnaces in Oct 1587: Jan Kapr with Dee's bricks (R2012a 506) vs Rožmberk
   (Parry 201).
8. Sericon vs antimony on 8 Feb 1588 (R2012a 505; EF 297 vs Parry 201).
9. Mortlake spoliation: mob legend vs creditors and associates (Parry 171–172).
10. The date of the Dunstan fire: 1587 (Fenton 231) vs "1597" (Whitby 159–160, likely a slip).
11. Dee's *Compound* annotation date: 1595 (R2012a 500) vs 1597 (CRC 155).
12. Kelley's fall: debt and failure to prove his art (EF 285, 301–302) vs officially a duel, really a
    political prize for Catholic forces (Parry 216).
13. Kelley's arrest: April 1591 (EF 284) vs May 1591 (Parry 216).
14. De Lannoy in the Tower: 1565 (R2011 144) vs 1566–67 (Parry 77–78).
15. The 1591 Frankfurt *Monas*: pirated (Clulee 2005 211) vs republished by Dee (Parry 215).
16. Date of Dee's antimonial gloss in Ashmole 1486 pt. 5: early 17th c. (EF 340; R2014 28) vs MS
    dated 1575–1600 (CRC 198).
