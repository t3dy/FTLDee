Secondary Sources

The definitive political biography
Glyn Parry — The Arch-Conjuror of England (Yale, 2011)
The game's primary source. Thesis: magical expertise is simultaneously useful and incriminating; reputation is manufactured and weaponised; the failed offices are the career. 21 chapters, fully summarised in the corpus.
Most-cited source · FTLDee encounters reference by chapter
Angelic conversations and natural philosophy
Deborah Harkness — John Dee's Conversations with Angels (Cambridge, 1999)
Thesis: the angelic project is not credulity but an eschatological programme — an Adamic key before the end of Nature. Central to the Kelley relationship and the Ottoman Soyga argument.
Central to Acts V–VI
The library and reading politics
William Sherman — John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing (UMass, 1995)
The Mortlake library as a living working institution, not a monument. The marginalia as active evidence. The library mechanic in FTLDee draws directly from this.
Library screen · Book system design
Occultism and magical exaltation
György Szőnyi — John Dee's Occultism (SUNY, 2004)
Dee's symbolic natural philosophy as already magical in the Propaedeumata. The continental afterlife of the Monas. Reception as part of the object.
Acts III–V
Renaissance occultism and seeing
Håkan Håkansson — Seeing the Word (Lund, 2001)
Ritual magic, theology, and eschatology integrated. The Heptarchic system as a structured seeing-project.
Acts III–V
The angelic action records
Christopher Whitby — John Dee's Actions with Spirits (Routledge, 1988)
Edition of the spirit action records, 22 December 1581 to 23 May 1583. The source for the 117 spirit action summaries in DeeChunks.
Act V encounters
The Ottoman counterfactual
Matthew Melvin-Koushki — "Dr Dee's Ottoman Adventure" (Hellebore, 2021)
Dee and Ibn Turka as parallel intellectual courtiers. The Soyga's Bunian-Bistamian lineage. Murad III as the patron Dee never met. The framing source for FTLDee's counterfactual arc.
Ottoman path · career_transition encounter
Monas: alchemy and Pythagorean symbolism
Stephen Clucas — Articles in Aries and Ambix
Ambix 64.2: royal typographer Silvius and the Monas's diagrammatic presentation. Aries 10.2: Pythagorean number symbolism and disciplina noua.
Monas dispute · book data
Monas alchemical reception
Peter Forshaw — Article in Ambix 52.3
Early alchemical reception of the Monas through Khunrath, Libavius, and the Theatrum Chemicum. The continental afterlife.
Monas encounter
Monas and geometrical cabala
Michael Walton — Article in Ambix 23.2
The Monas as geometrical cabala. One of the three scholarly positions the game's Monas DISPUTED encounter presents.
