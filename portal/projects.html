Why Ibn Turka matters to FTLDee

Melvin-Koushki reads the two as counterparts: Ibn Turka a Timurid Dee, Dee an Elizabethan "Dr. Littleturk". Both sought a mathematical, magical, lettrist and alchemical unification of knowledge in the service of a millenarian empire; both were smeared in their own day. (Paraphrase of Melvin-Koushki 2021; the longer sentence often quoted from him is not found in any text on disk.)

The difference is institutional context: Ibn Turka operated in the Timurid Persianate world where occult-scientific synthesis was expected of advisers to philosopher-kings. Dee operated in Protestant England where it was suspect. The Ottoman counterfactual in FTLDee asks: what if Dee had found the court whose episteme matched his?

M-K's further argument: the Book of Soyga's lore derives from the Bunian-Bistamian corpus popular in Ottoman courts. This means the eastward thread appears in Dee's very first angelic sessions — before he knows it is there.
